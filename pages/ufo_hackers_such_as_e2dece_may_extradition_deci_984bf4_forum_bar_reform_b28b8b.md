## Introduction

The campaign to stop Gary McKinnon’s extradition did more than influence one high-profile hacking case. It became a catalyst for a long-running debate about where criminal cases should be tried when conduct, evidence and defendants are closely connected to the United Kingdom but another country—especially the United States—also claims jurisdiction. Out of that debate emerged the modern UK “forum bar”, a legal mechanism allowing courts to refuse extradition when justice is better served by a domestic prosecution. The [reform]({{ 'reform/' | relative_url }}) was closely associated with the political fallout from the McKinnon case and was introduced shortly after Theresa May blocked his extradition in 2012.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/gary-mckinnon-extradition-case-home-secretarys-statement" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gary mckinnon extradition case home secretarys statement</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gary McKinnon extradition case: Home Secretary&#x27;s statement16 Oct 2012 — I have concluded that Mr McKinnon&#x27;s extradition would give rise t...</span></span></span>

## Why forum became a public demand

For years, critics of UK extradition arrangements argued that British citizens could be sent abroad even when much of the alleged conduct occurred in Britain. The concern was not limited to McKinnon, but his case became the most recognisable example because he lived in London, carried out the alleged computer [intrusions]({{ 'intrusions/' | relative_url }}) from the UK, and yet faced prosecution in the United States. His supporters asked why a British defendant whose actions originated in Britain could not be tried in a British court.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm201012/cmselect/cmhaff/644/64403.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.parliament.uk">[UK Parliament]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Parliament The US-UK Extradition Treaty</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Committee has...Read more...</span></span></span>

The concept at the centre of the debate was “forum”: the most appropriate jurisdiction for a criminal trial. Critics argued that extradition law gave insufficient weight to the location of the defendant, evidence, witnesses and alleged conduct. Parliamentary discussions and reform campaigns repeatedly pointed to McKinnon’s case as evidence that courts needed a clearer power to ask whether a UK-linked case should remain in the UK.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ein.org.uk/news/house-commons-votes-through-motion-reform-extradition-laws" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ein.org.uk">[Electronic Immigration Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ein.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">house commons votes through motion reform extradition laws</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Electronic Immigration NetworkHouse of Commons agrees motion to reform extradition laws6 Dec 2011 — The lack of discretion to allow the U...</span></span></span>

The issue gained additional momentum because the argument was broader than sympathy for one individual. [Campaigners]({{ 'campaigners/' | relative_url }}) contended that even where extradition was legally available, there should be a safeguard against sending a person overseas when a realistic domestic prosecution could achieve justice. That concern became politically significant enough that reform of extradition law remained on the agenda even after reviews rejected claims that the UK–US treaty itself was fundamentally imbalanced.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://corkerbinning.com/scott-bakers-extradition-review-no-whitewash/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: corkerbinning.com">[Corker Binning]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">corkerbinning.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">scott bakers extradition review no whitewash</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Corker BinningScott Baker&#x27;s extradition review – no whitewash &#124; Insights19 Oct 2011 — The evidence cited at page 472 of the report sugges...</span></span></span>

## How the 2013 reform shifted decisions toward courts

When Theresa May announced her decision to halt McKinnon’s extradition in October 2012, she also outlined broader extradition reforms. Among them was the introduction of a strengthened forum bar, designed to allow courts to consider whether prosecution should occur in the United Kingdom rather than abroad.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-w-i-r-e-uk/lauri-love-the-forum-bar-shows-its-mettle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wilmerhale.com">[WilmerHale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wilmerhale.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">lauri love the forum bar shows its mettle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lauri Love: The Forum Bar Shows its Mettle16 Mar 2018 — May concluded that the extradition of McKinnon, who suffered from Asper...</span></span></span>

The reform was enacted through the Crime and Courts Act 2013, which inserted a new forum-bar provision into the Extradition Act 2003. Under this framework, judges can stop extradition if a substantial part of the relevant activity occurred in the UK and if, considering the interests of justice, the case is more appropriately dealt with domestically.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ukhumanrightsblog.com/2018/02/09/the-forum-bar-it-does-exist-bruno-min/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ukhumanrightsblog.com">[UK Human Rights Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ukhumanrightsblog.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Human Rights Blog The Forum Bar: it does exist</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK Human Rights BlogThe Forum Bar: it does exist - Bruno Min9 Feb 2018 — These changes included a new &#x27;forum bar&#x27; to extradition, which g...</span></span></span>

The change was important for two reasons.

**First, it judicialised the question of forum.** Rather than leaving concerns about the proper venue solely to prosecutors or ministers, courts were given a statutory role in evaluating where a trial should take place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ukhumanrightsblog.com/2018/02/09/the-forum-bar-it-does-exist-bruno-min/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ukhumanrightsblog.com">[UK Human Rights Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ukhumanrightsblog.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Human Rights Blog The Forum Bar: it does exist</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK Human Rights BlogThe Forum Bar: it does exist - Bruno Min9 Feb 2018 — These changes included a new &#x27;forum bar&#x27; to extradition, which g...</span></span></span>

**Second, it focused on practical justice rather than nationality.** The forum bar does not simply protect British citizens. Instead, courts examine factors such as:

* Where most of the alleged conduct occurred.
* Where the evidence and witnesses are located.
* The interests of victims.
* Whether a domestic prosecution is realistically available.
* The overall interests of justice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ukhumanrightsblog.com/2018/02/09/the-forum-bar-it-does-exist-bruno-min/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ukhumanrightsblog.com">[UK Human Rights Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ukhumanrightsblog.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Human Rights Blog The Forum Bar: it does exist</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK Human Rights BlogThe Forum Bar: it does exist - Bruno Min9 Feb 2018 — These changes included a new &#x27;forum bar&#x27; to extradition, which g...</span></span></span>

The reform therefore addressed a specific criticism highlighted by McKinnon’s supporters: that extradition law needed a mechanism for asking whether a UK-centred case should remain before UK courts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uk.practicallaw.thomsonreuters.com/3-524-3488?contextData=%28sc.Default%29&amp;transitionType=Default" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uk.practicallaw.thomsonreuters.com">[Practical Law]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uk.practicallaw.thomsonreuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Practical LawExtradition and prosecution: raising the forum bar?Following the Gary McKinnon extradition case, the Home Secretary said tha...</span></span></span>

## What the change meant for future hacking cases

For several years after its introduction, some observers questioned whether the forum bar would have any practical effect. Early commentary suggested that courts might rarely use it and that prosecutors would continue to dominate decisions about venue.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022018320902544" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.sagepub.com">[Sage Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.sagepub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sage JournalsThe Forum Bar in UK Extradition Law: An Unnecessary...30 Jan 2020 — The introduction of the forum bar into UK extradition l...</span></span></span>

The provision eventually gained prominence through later cybercrime and hacking cases, most notably that of British activist and alleged hacker [Lauri Love]({{ 'lauri-love/' | relative_url }}). His extradition proceedings became one of the first major tests of the reformed system. Courts were required to examine whether alleged offences with substantial UK connections should be tried domestically rather than in the United States.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wired.com/2016/09/u-k-judge-oks-extradition-u-s-alleged-hacker-lauri-love" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Judge OKs Extradition to the U.S. for Hacktivist Lauri LoveSeptember 16, 2016 — British activist and alleged hacker Lauri Love will be ex...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 16, 2016</span></span></span>

## A reform that outlasted the McKinnon controversy

The significance of the forum bar lies in the fact that it addressed a concern exposed by the McKinnon battle without depending on the exceptional medical and [human-rights]({{ 'human-rights/' | relative_url }}) circumstances that ultimately stopped his extradition. Theresa May’s 2012 intervention was based on suicide risk and vulnerability, but the subsequent reform tackled a different question: where a cross-border criminal case ought to be heard.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/gary-mckinnon-extradition-case-home-secretarys-statement" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gary mckinnon extradition case home secretarys statement</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gary McKinnon extradition case: Home Secretary&#x27;s statement16 Oct 2012 — I have concluded that Mr McKinnon&#x27;s extradition would give rise t...</span></span></span>
