## Introduction

One of the clearest cybersecurity [lessons]({{ 'lessons/' | relative_url }}) associated with the Gary McKinnon case is that administrator accounts should never be treated like ordinary user accounts. A standard user account can usually access only a limited set of systems and data. An administrator account, by contrast, can alter configurations, create new accounts, install software, erase logs, and move across connected systems. When such an account is compromised, the consequences increase dramatically. Modern cybersecurity frameworks therefore treat privileged accounts as a separate risk category that requires stronger controls, tighter monitoring, and stricter governance than everyday user logins. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/least_privilege" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: csrc.nist.gov">[NIST Computer Security Resource Center+2CISA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">csrc.nist.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Computer Security Resource Centerleast privilege</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NIST Computer Security Resource Centerleast privilege - Glossary &#124; CSRCDefinitions: A security principle that a system should restrict th...</span></span></span>

## Why Privileged Credentials Change the Impact of Compromise

A privileged account exists to perform powerful actions. System administrators, database administrators, cloud administrators, and certain service accounts are deliberately granted authority that ordinary users do not possess. Because these accounts can change how systems operate, attackers often seek them first. Modern privileged-access guidance defines these accounts as identities with elevated permissions capable of controlling systems, modifying configurations, or managing other users. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wallix.com/blogpost/what-is-privileged-access-management-pam/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wallix.com">[WALLIX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wallix.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What is Privileged Access Management (PAM)?A privileged account is a user, service, or system account with elevated permissions tha...</span></span></span>

The McKinnon case illustrates why this distinction matters. According to US charging documents, investigators alleged that administrative privileges were obtained on numerous systems and then used to install remote-management software and additional tools. Once elevated access existed, a single compromised machine could become a platform for reaching other targets. * A compromised administrator account may expose an entire network.

## Controls That Separate Admin Access From Ordinary Use

The most effective organisations create a clear boundary between normal work and administrative work. Employees who administer systems often maintain separate accounts: one for email, web browsing, and office tasks, and another used only when administrative actions are required.

This separation reduces the likelihood that a phishing email, malicious website, or compromised workstation immediately exposes high-value credentials. It also makes monitoring more effective because administrative activity becomes easier to identify and audit. Government and industry guidance consistently recommends identifying privileged identities separately and applying enhanced controls to them. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.idmanagement.gov/playbooks/pam/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: idmanagement.gov">[idmanagement.gov+2Industrial Cyber]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">idmanagement.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Privileged Identity PlaybookDefine and identify privileged users as people, devices, and accounts with elevated access to an agency&#x27;s res...</span></span></span>

Common controls include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Multi-factor authentication (MFA)** for all privileged accounts.
* **Dedicated administrator workstations** used only for administrative tasks.
* **Session recording and logging** of privileged actions.
* **Approval workflows** before elevated access is granted.
* **Regular privilege reviews** to remove unnecessary permissions.
* **Separation of duties**, ensuring no single administrator controls every critical function. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://industrialcyber.co/cisa/nsa-cisa-publish-identity-and-access-management-recommended-best-practices-for-administrators/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: industrialcyber.co">[Cisco Duo+3Industrial Cyber+3NIST Publications]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">industrialcyber.co</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Industrial CyberNSA, CISA publish Identity and Access Management...22 Mar 2023 — The paper identifies key mitigations to the top threats...</span></span></span>

## Why Least Privilege Matters

A key principle behind modern privileged-access management is "least privilege". NIST defines least privilege as restricting users and processes to the minimum access necessary to perform assigned tasks. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/least_privilege" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: csrc.nist.gov">[NIST Computer Security Resource Center]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">csrc.nist.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Computer Security Resource Centerleast privilege</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NIST Computer Security Resource Centerleast privilege - Glossary &#124; CSRCDefinitions: A security principle that a system should restrict th...</span></span></span>

The idea sounds simple, but it directly addresses a failure pattern seen repeatedly in major [intrusions]({{ 'intrusions/' | relative_url }}). When users accumulate permissions over time, organisations create unnecessary attack paths. An account that only needs to manage one server may end up with rights across an entire environment. If that account is compromised, the attacker inherits all of those permissions.

In the context of the McKinnon case, the lesson is not merely that administrative access existed. The lesson is that elevated privileges create leverage. Once an attacker gains powerful credentials, the intrusion can expand far beyond the original entry point. Modern privileged-access programmes therefore focus on limiting both the number of privileged accounts and the scope of each account's authority. Least privilege also helps reduce [lateral movement]({{ 'lateral-movement/' | relative_url }})—the process by which an intruder moves from one system to another after gaining initial access. By restricting permissions, organisations reduce the number of pathways available to an attacker. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.techdemocracy.com/resources/privileged-access-management-and-nist-220" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: techdemocracy.com">[TechDemocracy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">techdemocracy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">privileged access management and nist 220</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cloud Environments and Privileged Access.Read more...</span></span></span>

## How Time-Limited Privileges Reduce Standing Risk

One of the most important developments since the early 2000s is the move away from permanent administrator rights.

Historically, many administrators retained elevated access at all times. While convenient, this approach created a standing risk: if the account was ever compromised, the attacker immediately inherited full administrative authority.

Modern privileged-access management increasingly uses time-limited or "just-in-time" access. Under this model, administrative rights are granted only when required for a specific task and are automatically removed afterwards. ## The Lasting Governance Lesson

The McKinnon case is often remembered because of its UFO-related motivation, but the enduring security lesson is far more practical. Sensitive organisations cannot rely on the assumption that administrator accounts will always be used correctly or remain uncompromised. The authority attached to those accounts is simply too great.

Modern privileged-access management emerged from exactly this recognition. Administrative credentials receive stronger authentication, more extensive monitoring, narrower permissions, and increasingly temporary access windows because the consequences of compromise are fundamentally different from those associated with ordinary users. When an organisation treats privileged accounts as just another login, a minor access failure can become a major incident. When it treats them as a distinct category of risk, the damage from inevitable mistakes becomes much harder to amplify. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nccoe.nist.gov/financial-services/privileged-account-management" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nccoe.nist.gov">[BeyondTrust+3NCCoE+3idmanagement.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nccoe.nist.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Privileged Account Management for the Financial Services...Organizations can use the Privileged Account Management NIST Cybersecuri...</span></span></span>
