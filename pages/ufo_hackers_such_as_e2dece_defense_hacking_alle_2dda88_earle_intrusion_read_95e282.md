## Introduction

Within the wider allegations against UFO-motivated hacker Gary McKinnon, the intrusion at Naval Weapons Station Earle attracted unusual attention because prosecutors argued it involved far more than a routine military office network. Earle was, and remains, a key Navy logistics installation responsible for supplying ordnance and other materiel to Atlantic Fleet forces. According to US authorities, systems at the base helped track the status, readiness and resupply needs of naval vessels. ## The Role of Naval Weapons Station Earle

Naval Weapons Station Earle in New Jersey is a major ordnance and logistics facility supporting Atlantic Fleet operations. The installation originated as a wartime ammunition depot and continues to serve as a hub for storing, processing and transferring naval munitions. Official Navy descriptions identify its mission as supporting Atlantic Fleet and broader Department of Defense requirements while providing logistics and force-protection services to visiting and homeported ships.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.navfac.navy.mil/Divisions/Environmental/Products-and-Services/Environmental-Restoration/Mid-Atlantic/Earle-NWS/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: navfac.navy.mil">[navfac.navy.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">navfac.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Earle NWS</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval Facilities Engineering Systems CommandThe current mission of Earle is to operate and maintain a coastal ordnance handling and proce...</span></span></span>

That mission explains why prosecutors repeatedly highlighted Earle when describing the alleged hacking campaign. The House of Lords judgment summarising the US case specifically noted that the station was responsible for replenishing munitions and supplies for the deployed Atlantic Fleet. Unlike a purely administrative network, Earle sat close to the Navy's supply chain and operational support structure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200708/ldjudgmt/jd080730/mckinn-1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.parliament.uk">[UK Parliament]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">mckinn 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK ParliamentMckinnon V Government of The United States of America...30 Jul 2008 — 26 navy computers, including US Naval Weapons Station...</span></span></span>

The emphasis on logistics is important because military readiness depends not only on combat systems but also on the ability to track ships, move ammunition and coordinate resupply. A disruption at a facility tasked with those functions naturally appears more serious than a breach of an isolated office system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.navfac.navy.mil/Divisions/Environmental/Products-and-Services/Environmental-Restoration/Mid-Atlantic/Earle-NWS/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: navfac.navy.mil">[navy.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">navfac.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Earle NWS</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval Facilities Engineering Systems CommandThe current mission of Earle is to operate and maintain a coastal ordnance handling and proce...</span></span></span>

## Why Prosecutors Focused on One Particular Computer

The most frequently cited allegation concerned a machine known as the [Port Services]({{ 'port-services/' | relative_url }}) computer. According to the New Jersey indictment, this was the primary computer used by Naval Weapons Station Earle to monitor the identity, location, physical condition, staffing, battle readiness and resupply status of Navy ships operating in or near the station's pier complex. Prosecutors alleged that McKinnon gained access to this computer in April 2001 and installed remote-control software known as [RemotelyAnywhere]({{ 'remotelyanywhere/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Department of Justice British National Charged with Hacking Into N.J</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">are under way to extradite McKinnon from the United Kingdom to stand trial on the charge, said U.S. Attorney Christie. From the prosecution's perspective, the importance of the machine was not merely that it belonged to the Navy. The concern was that it allegedly provided visibility into operational fleet-support information. The House of Lords summary of the extradition case likewise referred to a system used for monitoring the identity, location, physical condition, staffing and battle readiness of naval vessels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200708/ldjudgmt/jd080730/mckinn-1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.parliament.uk">[UK Parliament]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">mckinn 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK ParliamentMckinnon V Government of The United States of America...30 Jul 2008 — 26 navy computers, including US Naval Weapons Station...</span></span></span>

## Password Theft Allegations

Another major element of the Earle allegations involved password files. The New Jersey [indictment]({{ 'indictment/' | relative_url }}) alleged that between 18 and 21 June 2001 McKinnon repeatedly accessed the Port Services system and used previously installed software to obtain roughly 950 passwords stored on servers connected to the Earle network. The significance of the password allegation was not simply the number itself. Prosecutors argued that access to large collections of credentials could enable broader movement through military networks and potentially allow continued access even after the initial intrusion had been discovered. ## Readiness and Resupply Concerns

## The Debate Over Harm

        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {

        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
