## Introduction

Lauri Love’s extradition battle became the clearest test of how far the [lessons]({{ 'lessons/' | relative_url }}) of Gary McKinnon’s case would reach. Both men were British computer hackers accused by the United States of serious cyber offences. Both were diagnosed with Asperger syndrome (now generally classified within autism spectrum disorder), both faced arguments about severe depression and suicide risk, and both became symbols in wider debates over the UK–US extradition system. Yet Love’s victory in 2018 did not simply repeat the outcome of McKinnon’s case. Instead, it showed how McKinnon had influenced the legal landscape while also demonstrating that courts would still require detailed, case-specific evidence before blocking extradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7476620/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCAutism spectrum disorder and suitability for extradition</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">by I Freckelton · 2020 · Cited by 8 — The effects of the extradition bar and the legal sequelae of the previous judicial decisions in...</span></span></span>

## Why Love's Case Drew Comparisons to McKinnon

The similarities were obvious from the outset. Like McKinnon, Love was a British citizen accused of hacking US government systems from the United Kingdom. Both faced prosecution in America for cyber offences that allegedly affected US federal agencies. Both argued that extradition would expose them to extreme psychological harm because of autism-related vulnerabilities and associated mental-health conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7476620/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[nih.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCAutism spectrum disorder and suitability for extradition</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">by I Freckelton · 2020 · Cited by 8 — The effects of the extradition bar and the legal sequelae of the previous judicial decisions in...</span></span></span>

Public discussion quickly framed Love as “the next McKinnon”. Campaigners, MPs and civil-liberties groups argued that the same concerns raised in McKinnon’s case remained unresolved: whether a vulnerable British defendant should be removed to another country when much of the alleged conduct occurred in the UK and when a domestic prosecution might be possible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/law/2016/nov/14/amber-rudd-approves-lauri-love-extradition-to-us-on-hacking-charges" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Love, who could face up to 99 years in prison if convicted, argues that his health conditions, including depression and eczema, could wor...</span></span></span>

The comparison became even stronger because McKinnon’s case had triggered political reforms. Theresa May’s decision to halt McKinnon’s extradition in 2012 helped drive changes to UK extradition law, including the introduction of the so-called “[forum bar]({{ 'forum-bar/' | relative_url }})”, a mechanism allowing courts to consider whether justice would be better served by a trial in the UK. Love’s appeal became the first major case to test that [reform]({{ 'reform/' | relative_url }}) in practice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.newlawjournal.co.uk/content/forum-bar-prevents-extradition" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newlawjournal.co.uk">[newlawjournal.co.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newlawjournal.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">forum bar prevents extradition</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Law JournalForum bar prevents extradition6 Feb 2018 — The case, Love v Government of USA [2018] EWHC 172 (Admin), is the first succes...</span></span></span>

## Where the Legal and Health Arguments Overlapped

### Autism, vulnerability and suicide risk

The most striking overlap involved psychiatric evidence. In McKinnon’s case, Theresa May concluded that extradition would create such a high risk of suicide that it would be incompatible with his [human rights]({{ 'human-rights/' | relative_url }}). His Asperger syndrome, depression and vulnerability to the stress of extradition were central to that assessment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/news/theresa-may-statement-on-gary-mckinnon-extradition" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">theresa may statement on gary mckinnon extradition</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">16 Oct 2012 — I have concluded that Mr McKinnon&#x27;s extradition would give rise to such a high risk of him ending his life that a decision...</span></span></span>

Love advanced similar arguments. Expert witnesses told the courts that he was autistic, suffered from depression and faced a substantial risk of suicide if extradited. Judicial findings accepted that the risk was genuine and serious. The High Court ultimately concluded that his mental condition made extradition oppressive, particularly given concerns about how he would cope with imprisonment and isolation in the United States.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/lauri-love-v-usa.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: judiciary.uk">[judiciary.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">judiciary.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Courts and Tribunals JudiciaryLauri Love -v- The Government of the United States of...February 5, 2018 — 5 Feb 2018 — There will be a hi...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 5, 2018</span></span></span>

Legal and psychiatric commentary later identified both cases as major examples of courts grappling with how autism spectrum conditions can affect extradition decisions. The common theme was not that autism itself barred extradition, but that autism-related vulnerabilities could significantly affect the human-rights analysis when combined with evidence of severe mental distress and suicide risk.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7476620/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCAutism spectrum disorder and suitability for extradition</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">by I Freckelton · 2020 · Cited by 8 — The effects of the extradition bar and the legal sequelae of the previous judicial decisions in...</span></span></span>

### The question of where a hacker should be tried

A second overlap concerned jurisdiction. Supporters of both men argued that the alleged conduct largely occurred from computers located in Britain and that any prosecution should therefore take place in the UK rather than overseas. This issue had been politically prominent during McKinnon’s campaign and became legally significant in Love’s appeal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://corkerbinning.com/will-love-change-everything-extradition-and-the-forum-bar/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: corkerbinning.com">[Corker Binning]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">corkerbinning.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Corker Binning Will Love change everything?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Extradition and the forum bar5 Feb 2018 — Love was the first high profile case to properly test the effectiveness of the forum bar to ext...</span></span></span>

In Love’s case, the courts gave substantial weight to the possibility of prosecution in Britain. Judges examined where the conduct occurred, where evidence was located, and whether the interests of justice required a US trial. This analysis became central to the successful use of the forum bar.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ukhumanrightsblog.com/2018/02/09/the-forum-bar-it-does-exist-bruno-min/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ukhumanrightsblog.com">[ukhumanrightsblog.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ukhumanrightsblog.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Human Rights Blog The Forum Bar: it does exist</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK Human Rights BlogThe Forum Bar: it does exist - Bruno MinFebruary 9, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 9, 2018</span></span></span>

## Why McKinnon Was Not a Blanket Rule

Although Love ultimately avoided extradition, the courts were careful not to treat McKinnon as creating an automatic exemption for autistic defendants or hacking cases. Legal commentators repeatedly noted that McKinnon’s outcome was highly fact-specific and depended on unusual evidence about his mental [health and suicide]({{ 'health-argument/' | relative_url }}) risk.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7476620/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCAutism spectrum disorder and suitability for extradition</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">by I Freckelton · 2020 · Cited by 8 — The effects of the extradition bar and the legal sequelae of the previous judicial decisions in...</span></span></span>

Love’s case illustrated that point. He did not win simply because McKinnon had won. Instead, judges conducted a detailed examination of his own psychiatric evidence, the likely consequences of extradition, and the operation of the forum bar. The High Court stressed that the forum bar would only succeed in limited circumstances and that each extradition case required an individual assessment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-w-i-r-e-uk/lauri-love-the-forum-bar-shows-its-mettle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wilmerhale.com">[wilmerhale.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wilmerhale.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">lauri love the forum bar shows its mettle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lauri Love: The Forum Bar Shows its Mettle16 Mar 2018 — On 5 February 2017, the UK High Court issued judgment in the case of Lo...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 2017</span></span></span>

## The Lasting Significance of the Comparison

The McKinnon and Love cases are often linked because they exposed the same tensions: cybercrime with international consequences, competing UK and US claims to jurisdiction, and the challenge of balancing extradition obligations against severe mental-[health risks]({{ 'health-risk/' | relative_url }}). Yet the relationship between the two cases is best understood as evolutionary rather than identical. McKinnon's case prompted political and legal changes; Love's case tested those changes in court.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://corkerbinning.com/will-love-change-everything-extradition-and-the-forum-bar/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: corkerbinning.com">[Corker Binning]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">corkerbinning.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Corker Binning Will Love change everything?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Extradition and the forum bar5 Feb 2018 — Love was the first high profile case to properly test the effectiveness of the forum bar to ext...</span></span></span>
