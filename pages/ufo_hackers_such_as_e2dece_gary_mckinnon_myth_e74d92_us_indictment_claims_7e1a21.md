## Introduction

The most important fact about the Gary McKinnon case is that the legal case brought by US authorities was not a UFO case. The indictments and extradition proceedings centred on allegations of unauthorised access to government computers, damage to computer systems, deletion of files, theft of credentials and disruption to military networks. While McKinnon became famous for claiming he was searching for evidence of UFOs and secret technologies, those claims were largely irrelevant to the criminal allegations. US prosecutors treated the matter as a serious computer-crime and national-security case involving military and NASA systems, not as an investigation into what he said he was looking for.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeLondon, England Hacker Indicted Under Computer Fraud...Gary McKinnon, of London, England, was indicted in Alexandri...</span></span></span>

## What Was Gary McKinnon Actually Accused Of?

In November 2002, a federal grand jury in the Eastern District of Virginia indicted McKinnon on multiple computer-related offences. The US Department of Justice stated that he had conducted a prolonged campaign of unauthorised access against military and government networks between 2001 and 2002. According to prosecutors, he gained access to protected computers, obtained administrative privileges, copied password files and installed software that allowed continued remote access.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeLondon, England Hacker Indicted Under Computer Fraud...Gary McKinnon, of London, England, was indicted in Alexandri...</span></span></span>

The allegations were framed as deliberate [intrusions]({{ 'intrusions/' | relative_url }}) rather than casual browsing. Prosecutors claimed that McKinnon systematically scanned for vulnerable systems, exploited weak security controls and entered networks belonging to defence and civilian agencies. The indictment alleged not only unauthorised access but also intentional damage to protected computers and interference with government operations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeLondon, England Hacker Indicted Under Computer Fraud...Gary McKinnon, of London, England, was indicted in Alexandri...</span></span></span>

The affected systems were not described merely as ordinary office computers. Court records referred to computers associated with the Army's [Military District]({{ 'army-outage-c4f924/' | relative_url }}) of Washington network and systems connected to the Naval Weapons Station Earle facility in New Jersey, a site responsible for supplying munitions and support to naval forces. ## What Damage and Disruption Did Prosecutors Allege?

The most serious allegations involved disruption rather than espionage.

US authorities alleged that McKinnon deleted critical operating-system files and user accounts after gaining administrative control of some systems. According to court records, deletion of key files shut down the US Army's Military District of Washington network—comprising more than 2,000 computers—for approximately 24 hours. Prosecutors argued that this significantly disrupted government functions and required extensive repair efforts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vlex.co.uk/vid/mckinnon-v-usa-818719549" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vlex.co.uk">[vlex.co.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vlex.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">v Lex Mc Kinnon v United States of America</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">McKinnon v United States of AmericaOctober 17, 2019 — 30 Jul 2008 — Having gained access to these computers the appellant deleted dat...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 17, 2019</span></span></span>

Another major allegation concerned the Naval Weapons Station [Earle network]({{ 'earle-network/' | relative_url }}). Court records state that logs and other data were deleted from systems at the facility, rendering a network of more than 300 computers inoperable and creating concerns about operational readiness shortly after the September 2001 terrorist attacks. Prosecutors portrayed this as one of the most consequential incidents in the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vlex.co.uk/vid/mckinnon-v-usa-818719549" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vlex.co.uk">[vlex.co.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vlex.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">v Lex Mc Kinnon v United States of America</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">McKinnon v United States of AmericaOctober 17, 2019 — 30 Jul 2008 — Having gained access to these computers the appellant deleted dat...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 17, 2019</span></span></span>

The Department of Justice also alleged that McKinnon copied account information and password files and installed remote-access tools that enabled continued entry into systems. Officials estimated the resulting investigation, recovery and repair costs at hundreds of thousands of dollars, with figures in government statements ranging from roughly $700,000 to $900,000.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[justice.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeLondon, England Hacker Indicted Under Computer Fraud...Gary McKinnon, of London, England, was indicted in Alexandri...</span></span></span>

### Why Authorities Considered the Case Serious

The government's position was that the issue was not simply trespassing on computer networks. Prosecutors argued that the alleged actions impaired military systems, forced network shutdowns and consumed substantial resources to restore affected computers.
